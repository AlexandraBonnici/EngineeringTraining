03 · Forces and friction

Why does the robot move forwards?

A turning wheel pushes backwards on the ground. Friction pushes the wheel, and robot, forwards.

1 · Motor applies a torque

The motor turns the wheel so its contact point tends to move backwards along the floor.

2 · Static friction provides the grip

The wheel pushes backwards on the ground; the ground exerts a forward frictional force on the wheel.

3 · Constant velocity motion

If the forward frictional force balances the opposing forces, the robot continues moving forwards at constant velocity.

05 · Understanding motion

Why does the robot rotate?

Opposite forces on opposite sides create a turning effect (torque).

What is torque?

Torque is the turning effect of a force about a pivot or axis. Its magnitude depends on the force and its perpendicular distance from the pivot.

Torque makes the robot rotate

The forces on the two wheels act on opposite sides of the robot. Both produce torque in the same rotational direction.

τ = F × L

For an equal-force couple, L is the separation between the force lines.

06 · From model to calculation

Wheel rotation ≠ robot turn angle

Wheel travel

s = NπD

D = wheel diameter. N = number of wheel revolutions.

Distance needed for a turn

s = (θ / 360°)πL

L = track width: the distance between the left and right wheels. For a turn on the spot, each wheel traces a circle of diameter L.

Equate distances and simplify

N = θL / (360D)

So, N is the wheel revolutions needed for a robot turn angle θ.

09 · Real machines

Four wheels, not two?

The two front wheels are driven by motors. The rear wheels roll freely, but can still resist sideways motion during a turn.

Front wheels · driven

The motors turn the left and right front wheels in opposite directions to steer the robot.

Rear wheels · unpowered

The rear wheels follow the robot’s movement; they are not driven by motors.

Turning scrub

If the rear wheels cannot swivel sideways, they may skid laterally as the robot turns. Swivelling castors behave differently.

09 · From model to movement

Programming the Robot to Move Forwards

VEX V5 Python · Complete example

from vex import *

brain = Brain()

# 1. Configure the two driven motors
left_motor = Motor(Ports.PORT1)
right_motor = Motor(Ports.PORT2)
left_motor.set_reversed(True)

# 2. Measure wheel and robot dimensions (mm)
wheel_travel = 314
track_width = 300
wheelbase = 200

# 3. Initialise the drivetrain
drivetrain = DriveTrain(left_motor, right_motor,
                        wheel_travel, track_width,
                        wheelbase, MM)

# 4. Set forward speed and distance
drivetrain.set_drive_velocity(30, PERCENT)
drivetrain.drive_for(FORWARD, 500, MM)

1 · Configure the motors

The motors are mounted on opposite sides of the robot. Reversing the left motor ensures that both wheels drive the robot forwards when given the same forward command.

2 · Describe the robot

Measure the wheel circumference, track width and wheelbase.

3 · Move forwards

30% is the chosen drive speed. 500 mm is the commanded forward travel distance. The rear wheels are unpowered.
